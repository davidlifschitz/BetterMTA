# Product Principles

1. The commuter has useful knowledge.
2. Preferred lines are maximized when feasible; omissions and impossibility must be explained.
3. Never hide the time cost of a preference.
4. Verify candidate routes rather than trusting one heuristic path.
5. Familiar first, powerful second.
6. Every constraint is reversible in one tap.
7. Show the top three; do not bury the user in route permutations.
8. Live-data uncertainty must be visible.
9. A partial match is better than a dead end.
10. Better routes matter more than novelty.
11. Fill the gaps: unselected connectors may complete a trip without forcing the rider to name every piece.
